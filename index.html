      Race diary

        Step 1: be the driver

        Drive one full lap around the track. Hitting a wall is a bonk: you stop and lose time. Your best lap time is saved, and later the AI will try to beat it.

        Look at the yellow lines sticking out of your car. Each one measures how far away the wall is. Those, your speed, and your direction are everything an AI driver will ever know.

        Think about it

        1. When the "ahead" line gets short, what should you do?
        2. If "far left" is long and "far right" is short, which way is the road bending?
        3. Could you drive with your eyes closed if someone read you the 7 numbers?

        Step 2: how the brain thinks

        The 7 numbers go in on the left. Every circle does the same three things:

        1. Multiply each number coming in by its own weight.
        2. Add them all up.
        3. Squeeze the answer so it lands between −1 and +1.

        Layer after layer, until two circles come out. Gas: +1 is full gas, −1 is full brake. Steer: +1 is full right, −1 is full left.

        Counting the weights

        Each line is a weight, and each circle also has one extra number of its own, called a bias.

        7 × 16 + 16 = 128
        16 × 16 + 16 = 272
        16 × 2 + 2  = 34
        total = 434 weights

        Different weights make a different driver. Somewhere there's a set of 434 numbers that drives like a champion. Nobody knows them.

        Press New random brain a few times. Almost every one crashes. Then press Nudge the best brain, or turn on Auto: each next brain is a copy of the best one so far, with a few weights nudged at random. Some children are worse, some are better, and the better ones become the new parent. Watch the best brain creep further around the track.

        Step 3: survival of the fastest

        1. Make 20 random brains and let them all race.
        2. Most crash right away. Find the 5 that got furthest (or finished fastest).
        3. Copy them and nudge each weight a tiny bit at random. That makes 20 children.
        4. Some children are worse, some are better. The best become the new parents. Repeat!

        This is how nature works too. It's called evolution, and here it happens in seconds.

        What to watch for

        Early generations wiggle, stall, or drive straight into the first wall. Keep going. Somewhere around a few dozen generations, one car makes it all the way around. After that they get faster and faster.

        Nobody told them where the corners are or when to brake. Every line they drive, they found themselves. Use Max speed to fast-forward, then try the Champion ×20 parade, or go back to step 1 and race it.
